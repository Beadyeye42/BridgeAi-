# Bridge-iT: a profitable local-services pilot

## The first customer

Local service businesses with spare diary capacity. The supplier promise is: **Fill gaps in your diary with nearby jobs you actually want.** Buyer convenience supports that promise by producing clear, actionable requests. A larger lead count or radius is not itself evidence of value.

## Value for each participant

- Supplier: incremental earnings after labour, materials, travel, quoting time and Bridge-iT fees; control over availability, job size and area; a fair chance of winning.
- Buyer: a reliable provider, clear scope, understood pricing, realistic timing and a recorded outcome. Do not imply the cheapest quote is necessarily best.
- Bridge-iT: recurring income that covers payment processing, WhatsApp, AI, hosting, support and acquisition costs. Founder support time must be costed, even when no salary is taken.

## Pilot scope

Choose one compact area and one service category with short, verifiable completion cycles. Recruit a small group of genuinely available suppliers. Proposed learning target: five participating suppliers and twenty genuine completed jobs; these are pilot targets, not established benchmarks or proof of statistical significance. Do not count staff tests as customer demand. Do not expand until completed-job economics and supplier retention are understood.

Before a supplier quotes, the brief should establish the work required, approximate location, desired date/time window, relevant photos or measurements, site/access constraints, and whether the buyer wants an estimate or is ready to book. Mark missing information explicitly; never invent a budget or claim a buyer is verified merely because they supplied a phone number. Suppliers need a private pre-quote clarification path.

Show the actual maximum number of competing suppliers, not an invented scarcity counter. Never expose competing prices or customer identities before authorised release. Current request distribution supports a bounded shortlist; confirm the configured limit for each pilot request.

## Product gaps to close

The existing capability model has minimum order values, live availability and capacity; these should be easy to update. They are not a diary: explicit free date/time slots and buyer schedule matching still need implementation. Start with manual pilot coordination rather than pretending calendar bookings are persisted.

The dashboard should prioritise: update availability; inspect suitable jobs; respond to buyer questions; confirm selected work; record completion. Current selection is an expression of buyer intent, not confirmed revenue. Allow suppliers to decline unsuitable work without misleading performance penalties.

Measure the supplier's benefit before asking them to expand their radius. Free two-mile access is a trial of delivered value. Wider paid access should be offered with honest local demand evidence and no promise of guaranteed jobs. Retain current pricing until the conflicting pricing PRs are resolved and pilot evidence supports a change.

## Weekly scorecard

Track genuine requests, qualified requests, no-match requests, time to first useful response, quotations per request, selections, mutually confirmed jobs, completed jobs, cancellations, repeat buyers, and supplier continuation. Track quote preparation minutes and travel time, plus supplier-reported job contribution where they consent to share it. Do not equate turnover with profit.

Supplier net incremental contribution = job revenue minus incremental labour/material/travel costs, quoting effort and platform fees.

Bridge-iT contribution = collected subscription revenue minus refunds, payment fees and attributable WhatsApp/AI/hosting/support costs. Acquisition payback = acquisition cost divided by positive monthly contribution. If contribution is not positive, there is no finite payback under that model.

## Evidence as of 7 September 2026

The live database snapshot contains six suppliers (three approved), fifteen requests, nine quotations and seven selected requests; no requests are recorded as confirmed or completed. Records may include testing or incomplete follow-through. These counts do not establish commercial traction, revenue or profitability. First reconcile those outcomes with the owners; do not contact buyers/suppliers automatically.

## Decision gate

Continue only when participating suppliers can identify worthwhile additional completed work, buyers confirm satisfactory outcomes, and Bridge-iT can serve the cohort at a viable support cost. If suppliers do not win useful work, fix request quality, matching or local supply/demand before charging for more coverage. If buyers select but never confirm, investigate that transition before buying more traffic.
